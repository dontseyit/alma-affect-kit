    The Model¶

    ALMA (A Layered Model of Affect, Gebhard, AAMAS 2005) simulates three interacting kinds of affect as they occur in human beings, distinguished by how long they last and what they influence:

    Layer Duration Theory Bound to Role
    Emotions short-term (seconds) OCC (Ortony, Clore & Collins 1988) a specific event, action or object colour immediate reactions: facial expressions, gestures, wording
    Moods medium-term (minutes) Mehrabian's PAD temperament space nothing concrete, a longer-lasting stable affective state steer strategies: dialogue style, idle behaviour, posture
    Personality long-term (permanent) Big Five / Five Factor Model (McCrae & John 1992) the individual frames everything: the default mood and emotional dispositions

    How the layers interact¶

    world input ──► appraisal rules ──► EECs ──► OCC rules ──► active emotions
                                                                │ decay
             personality ──► emotion baselines ─────────────────┤
                                                                ▼
             personality ──► default mood          virtual emotion centre
                                  │                             │
                                  ▼           pull / push       ▼
                            mood return  ◄──────────────────► mood
    1. World input (events, acts, displays) is appraised through each character's subjective rules into emotion eliciting conditions (EECs).
    2. The OCC rules turn EECs into active emotions with intensities, biased by personality baselines. Active emotions decay back to their baseline within the emotion decay time (default 20s).
    3. All currently active emotions form a virtual emotion centre in PAD space, which drags the mood around via the pull/push mood change function.
    4. Absent active emotions, the mood slowly returns to the personality-derived default mood.

    Layer 1: Emotions (OCC)¶

    ALMA's "EmotionEngine" implements the OCC cognitive model of emotions. Here emotions are valenced reactions to events that concern the character, to actions of agents the character holds responsible, and to objects. Appraising these produces EEC variables. Their signs select among the 24 emotion types, and their absolute values determine intensity. See Appraisal for the full reference.

    Decay¶

    An elicited emotion decays from its initial intensity back to its personality baseline within the emotion decay time (20s by default). The decay curve is linear by default and can also be exponential, hyperbolicus, or a sigmoid that starts slowly, speeds up in the middle and levels off at the end.

    Dominant emotion¶

    Emotions should not a priori influence behaviour measurably: only the most intense active emotion whose intensity exceeds a threshold (default 0.4) is reported as dominant_emotion for behaviour control. All active emotions, dominant or not, still drive the mood.

    Layer 2: Moods (PAD space)¶

    Mood is a point in Mehrabian's three-dimensional pleasure–arousal–dominance space, each axis in [-1, 1]. The eight octants carry discrete mood names (paper, Table 1):
    +P+A+D Exuberant -P-A-D Bored
    +P+A-D Dependent -P-A+D Disdainful
    +P-A+D Relaxed -P+A-D Anxious
    +P-A-D Docile -P+A+D Hostile

    Mood strength is the Euclidean norm of the PAD vector. The longest distance inside an octant, √3, is divided into three equal parts: slightly [0, √3⁄3), moderate [√3⁄3, 2√3⁄3), fully [2√3⁄3, √3]. A mood of (0.25, −0.18, 0.12) reads slightly relaxed.

    The virtual emotion centre¶

    Every active emotion is a point in PAD space (via the table above). Their average position is the virtual emotion centre (VEC); its intensity is the average of the active emotions' intensities (max 1.0). The VEC jumps around erratically as emotions come and go, so it is not the mood itself, but the force that changes the mood smoothly.

    The pull and push mood change¶

    • Pull phase, while the current mood lies between the PAD origin and the VEC, the mood is attracted towards the VEC.
    • Push phase, once the mood is beyond (or at) the VEC, it is pushed further into its current octant. A person's mood intensifies as they undergo experiences that reinforce it. Repeated similar emotions therefore escalate a mood from slightly to moderate to fully.

    The intensity of the VEC determines the speed. At the maximum intensity setting, the mood travels the distance between the centers of two adjacent octants (1.0 PAD unit) within the mood transition time (which is 10 minutes by default)

    Mood return¶

    The mood tends to drift back to the default mood. The longest octant distance (√3) is travelled in the mood return time, 20 minutes by default. The return applies while no active emotions exist (can be changed by mood_return_mode="idle|always").

    Layer 3: Personality (Big Five)¶

    A character's personality is a Big Five profile, openness, conscientiousness, extraversion, agreeableness, neuroticism, each trait in [-1, 1]. It influences the model in two ways.

    Default mood¶

    Mehrabian's regression of the Big Five onto PAD defines the character's resting mood:

    Pleasure  =  0.21·Extraversion + 0.59·Agreeableness + 0.19·Neuroticism
Arousal   =  0.15·Openness + 0.30·Agreeableness − 0.57·Neuroticism
Dominance =  0.25·Openness + 0.17·Conscientiousness
             + 0.60·Extraversion − 0.32·Agreeableness

    The paper's worked example: openness 0.4, conscientiousness 0.8, extraversion 0.6, agreeableness 0.3, neuroticism 0.4 → default mood (P 0.38, A −0.08, D 0.50), octant Relaxed.

    Emotion baselines¶

    Personality biases emotion intensities with a baseline intensity per emotion type, "an extravert character's baseline intensity for joy is 0.15, whereas an introvert character's would be 0.0" (IVA'03). Elicited intensities are raised by the baseline, and emotions decay back to the baseline rather than to zero. The default weight table (positive emotions from extraversion + agreeableness, negative from neuroticism, small conscientiousness/openness terms for the attribution/attraction groups) is a documented interpolation from the papers' qualitative guidance, see Fidelity Notes.
